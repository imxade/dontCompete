**Alkalinity and pH of Water**
=====================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Alkalinity is a measure of water's capacity to resist changes in pH when acid or base is added. It plays a crucial role in determining the quality of water, especially in aquatic ecosystems. This theory note aims to provide an in-depth understanding of alkalinity and its relationship with pH.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### Alkalinity

Alkalinity (A) is defined as the sum of concentrations of hydroxide ions (OH^-), carbonate ions (CO_3^2-), bicarbonate ions (HCO_3^-), and other alkaline substances in water. It can be expressed as:

A = [OH^-] + [CO_3^2-] + 2[HCO_3^-]

where [ ] represents concentration in mol/litre.

### pH

pH is a measure of the concentration of hydrogen ions (H+) in water, with higher values indicating lower acidity. The pH scale ranges from 0 to 14, where:

* pH < 7: Acidic
* pH = 7: Neutral
* pH > 7: Basic (or alkaline)

### Equilibrium Constants

The following equilibrium constants are essential for understanding alkalinity and pH:

* K_W = [H^+][OH^-] = 10^(-14)
* K_HCO_3 = [H^+][CO_3^2-] / [HCO_3^-] = 10^(-10.33)
* K_CO_3 = [H^+][HCO_3^-] / [CO_3^2-] = 10^(-10.33)

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

### Alkalinity Calculation

Given the concentrations of HCO_3^- and CO_3^2-, the alkalinity (A) can be calculated using the following formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= [HCO_3^-] + \frac{K_HCO_3}{[H^+]} \\
&= 10^{-8.04} + \frac{10^{-10.33}}{10^{-9.0}}
\end{aligned}
$$

### pH and Alkalinity Relationship

The relationship between pH and alkalinity is governed by the following equation:

pH = -log[H^+] ≈ log(A/2)

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

* **Neglecting OH^- and H+**: When concentrations of OH^- and H+ are negligible, the alkalinity (A) can be approximated as:
A ≈ [CO_3^2-] + 2[HCO_3^-]

**Examples with Solutions**
-------------------------

### Example 1

Given: Concentration of HCO_3^- = 10^-8.04 mol/litre, concentration of CO_3^2- = 3.04 × 10^-9 mol/litre, and pH = 9.0.

Find: Alkalinity (A) in mg/litre as CaCO_3

Solution:

First, calculate the alkalinity using the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= [HCO_3^-] + \frac{K_HCO_3}{[H^+]} \\
&= 10^{-8.04} + \frac{10^{-10.33}}{10^{-9.0}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the given values and simplifying, we get:

A ≈ 10^-8.04 + 2 × 3.04 × 10^-9
≈ 1.21 × 10^(-7) mol/litre

To convert alkalinity to mg/litre as CaCO_3, multiply by the equivalent weight of CaCO_3 (100 g/mol):

A ≈ 1.21 × 10^(-7) mol/litre × 50
≈ 130.4 mg/litre

### Example 2

Given: Concentration of HCO_3^- = 5 × 10^-8 mol/litre, concentration of CO_3^2- = 2 × 10^-9 mol/litre, and pH = 7.

Find: Alkalinity (A) in mg/litre as CaCO_3

Solution:

First, calculate the alkalinity using the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= [HCO_3^-] + \frac{K_HCO_3}{[H^+]} \\
&= 5 × 10^{-8} + \frac{10^{-10.33}}{10^{-7}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the given values and simplifying, we get:

A ≈ 5 × 10^-8 + 2 × 10^-3
≈ 0.002 mol/litre

To convert alkalinity to mg/litre as CaCO_3, multiply by the equivalent weight of CaCO_3 (100 g/mol):

A ≈ 0.002 mol/litre × 50
≈ 100 mg/litre

**Common Pitfalls**
-------------------

* Failing to neglect OH^- and H+ when their concentrations are negligible.
* Not converting alkalinity from mol/litre to mg/litre as CaCO_3.
