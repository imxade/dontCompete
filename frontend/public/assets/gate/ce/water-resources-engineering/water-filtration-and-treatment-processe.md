**Water Filtration and Treatment Processes**
=============================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Water filtration and treatment processes are crucial in ensuring safe drinking water supply for communities. The goal of these processes is to remove contaminants, improve taste, odor, and color, and make water suitable for consumption. In this theory note, we will cover the key concepts, formulas, and insights required to solve questions related to water filtration and treatment.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### 1. Filtration Mechanisms

Filtration occurs when a fluid (water) passes through a porous medium (filtration bed). The two primary mechanisms involved in filtration are:

*   **Mechanical Straining**: Large particles are removed by physical straining.
*   **Interception**: Particles larger than the pore size are trapped.

### 2. Filtration Laws

The rate of filtration is governed by Darcy's Law, which states that:

$$Q = \frac{kA\Delta P}{\mu L}$$

where:

*   $Q$ is the flow rate
*   $k$ is the permeability coefficient
*   $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the filter bed
*   $\Delta P$ is the pressure drop across the filter bed
*   $\mu$ is the dynamic viscosity of water
*   $L$ is the length of the filter bed

### 3. Filtration Efficiency

Filtration efficiency is measured by the removal rate of suspended solids, which can be expressed as:

$$\eta = \frac{C_i - C_e}{C_i}$$

where:

*   $\eta$ is the filtration efficiency
*   $C_i$ is the initial concentration of suspended solids
*   $C_e$ is the final concentration of suspended solids after filtration

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

### 1. Terminal Settling Velocity

The terminal settling velocity of a particle can be calculated using Stokes' Law:

$$v_t = \frac{d^2g(\rho_p - \rho_w)}{18\mu}$$

where:

*   $v_t$ is the terminal settling velocity
*   $d$ is the diameter of the particle
*   $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity
*   $\rho_p$ is the density of the particle
*   $\rho_w$ is the density of water
*   $\mu$ is the dynamic viscosity of water

### 2. Hydraulic Backwash Velocity

The hydraulic backwash velocity can be calculated using:

$$v_b = \frac{d^2g(\rho_p - \rho_w)}{18\mu}$$

*   **Apply relevant formulas**: Use Darcy's Law, Stokes' Law, or other applicable equations to solve for the unknown variable.
*   **Consider unit conversions**: Ensure that all units are consistent and converted appropriately.

**Examples with Solutions**
---------------------------

### Example 1: Filtration Efficiency

Suppose we have a water filtration system that removes 80% of suspended solids. If the initial concentration is 100 mg/L, what is the final concentration after filtration?

$$\eta = \frac{C_i - C_e}{C_i} = 0.8$$
$$C_e = C_i(1-\eta) = 100(1-0.8) = 20$$

The final concentration of suspended solids after filtration is 20 mg/L.

### Example 2: Terminal Settling Velocity

Calculate the terminal settling velocity of a sand particle with a diameter of 0.4 mm, specific gravity of 2.55, and dynamic viscosity of water as 1.002 x 10^-3 Pa·s.

$$v_t = \frac{d^2g(\rho_p - \rho_w)}{18\mu} = \frac{(0.0004)^2(9.81)(2.55-1000)}{18(1.002 \times 10^{-3})}$$
$$v_t \approx 0.0075 \text{ m/s}$$
