# Irrigation
================

## Introduction
------------

Irrigation refers to the artificial application of water to land and crops, primarily for agriculture, but also for other uses like landscape and recreation. In the context of Water Resources Engineering, irrigation is a crucial aspect of managing water resources efficiently.

## Core Concepts
---------------

### Cross-Drainage Structures

Cross-drainage structures are designed to manage the interaction between natural drains (rivers or streams) and irrigation canals. There are four primary types:

*   **Aqueduct**: A structure that carries a canal over a natural drain.
*   **Super-Passage**: A structure where the canal passes above the natural drain, but without an aqueduct.
*   **Siphon Aqueduct**: A type of aqueduct where the water flows under gravity through a siphon.
*   **Level Crossing**: A structure that allows the canal to cross over or under the natural drain at the same level.

### Water Flow and Cross-Drainage

When designing irrigation systems, it's essential to consider the relationship between the canal's bottom level (HFL - Highest Flood Level) and the natural drain. The correct positioning of these structures is critical for efficient water management.

No specific formulae or equations are provided in this topic. However, understanding the principles behind cross-drainage structures and irrigation systems is crucial.
