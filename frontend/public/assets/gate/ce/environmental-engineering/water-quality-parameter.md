**Water Quality Parameters**
==========================

### Introduction
-----------------

Water quality parameters are essential indicators of water suitability for various uses, including drinking, irrigation, and industrial processes. In this note, we'll focus on alkalinity, a crucial parameter in water treatment.

### Core Concepts
------------------

#### Alkalinity Definition
Alkalinity is the capacity of water to neutralize acids. It's typically measured as mg/L (as CaCO3).

**CaCO3**: Calcium carbonate

**HCO3-**: Bicarbonate ion

**OH-**: Hydroxide ion

**H+**: Hydrogen ion

#### Chemical Equilibrium
In aqueous solutions, water reacts with CO2 to form carbonic acid (H2CO3), which dissociates into H+ and HCO3-. Alkalinity is the sum of all bases that can neutralize H+, including OH-.

#### Relevant Reactions

*   **Carbon dioxide reaction**:

    $ CO_{2} + H_{2}O \rightleftharpoons H^{+} + HCO_{3}^{-}$

*   **Bicarbonate dissociation**:


    $$HCO^{-}_{3} \rightleftharpoons H^{+} + CO^{2-}_{3}$$


#### Key Formulas/Theorems
---------------------------------

**Alkalinity calculation**: $Alkalinity = \frac{1000 \times (40 \times Ca^{2+}) + (12 \times C^{4+}) + (16 \times O^{2-})}{50}$

where $Ca^{2+}=$ calcium ion, $C^{4+}=$ carbon ion, and $O^{2-}=$ oxide ion.

**Calcium bicarbonate molar mass**: $M_{HCO^{-}_{3}} = 40 + 12 + (16 \times 3) = 100$ g/mol

**Alum dosage**:
The quantity of alum ($Al_{2}(SO_{4})_{3} \cdot 14H_{2}O$) required for coagulation can be estimated using the following formula:

$Q_{alum} = Q_{water} \times C_{alum} \times \frac{M_{alum}}{M_{HCO^{-}_{3}}}$
where $Q_{water}$ is the water flow rate, $C_{alum}$ is the alum concentration, and $M_{alum}$ and $M_{HCO^{-}_{3}}$ are the molar masses of alum and calcium bicarbonate, respectively.

### Examples with Solutions
---------------------------

**Example 1**

Q: A water treatment plant treats 25 MLD water with a natural alkalinity of 4.0 mg/L (as CaCO3). It's estimated that, during coagulation of this water, 450 kg/day of calcium bicarbonate is required based on the alum dosage.

S:

1.  Calculate the molar mass of calcium bicarbonate: $M_{HCO^{-}_{3}} = 100$ g/mol
2.  Determine the quantity of pure quick lime (CaO) required for this process per day:

    *   $Q_{alum} = \frac{450 \times 10^{3}}{100} = 4500\,kg/day$
    *   Since the molar mass of CaO is 56 g/mol, we can find the quantity of quick lime required:

        $Q_{CaO} = Q_{alum} \times \frac{M_{CaO}}{M_{HCO^{-}_{3}}}$
        $Q_{CaO} = 4500\,kg/day \times \frac{56}{100} = 252\,kg/day$

    **Note:** This example is for illustration purposes and may not match the exact numbers in the provided question.

### Common Pitfalls
----------------------

1.  **Incorrect molar mass calculations**: Double-check the molar masses of relevant compounds.
2.  **Inconsistent units**: Ensure consistent units throughout the calculation.
