**Theodolites and Plane Table Surveying**
=====================================

### Introduction
-----------------

A theodolite is a precise surveying instrument used to measure angles between visible objects, typically on the Earth's surface. It consists of a telescope with two vertical circles (altitude and azimuth) and two spirit levels (plate level and altitude level). The plane table surveying method uses a combination of trigonometric principles and plane table coordinates to determine positions and orientations of features.

### Core Concepts
-----------------

#### **Fundamental Lines of a Transit Theodolite**

The fundamental lines of a transit theodolite are:

*   Vertical axis (V): passes through the center of the instrument, perpendicular to the horizontal axis.
*   Horizontal axis (H): intersects the vertical axis at its midpoint, dividing the instrument into two equal parts.
*   Axis of altitude level: lies in a plane perpendicular to the vertical axis and parallel to the horizontal axis.
*   Plate level axis: lies in a plane parallel to the vertical axis.

**Key Principle:** The fundamental lines are used to establish the reference system for measurements.

#### **Zenith Angle and Vertical Angle**

The zenith angle (Z) is measured between the plumb line of the instrument and the object being observed. It is related to the vertical angle (V) by:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Vertical Angle} &= 90^\circ - \text{Zenith Angle}\\
&= 90^\circ - Z\\
&= 0 A V 3 = 0 A Z V 90 + 
\end{aligned}
$$

**Key Formula:** The vertical angle is calculated by subtracting the zenith angle from 90 degrees.

### Problem Solving Patterns
------------------------------

*   **Using the Fundamental Lines:**

    When setting up a transit theodolite, ensure that:
    *   The vertical axis is perpendicular to the horizontal axis at its intersection.
    *   The axis of altitude level is perpendicular to the line of collimation.

### Examples with Solutions
---------------------------

#### Example 1:

A surveyor measures a zenith angle of $93^\circ\ 00^\prime\ 00^{\prime\prime}$ during a theodolite survey. What is the corresponding vertical angle?

**Solution:**

Using the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Vertical Angle} &= 90^\circ - \text{Zenith Angle}\\
&= 90^\circ - 93^\circ\\
&= -3^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the vertical angle is $-3^\circ$.

#### Example 2:

A surveyor observes a zenith angle of $27^\circ\ 30^\prime\ 00^{\prime\prime}$. What is the corresponding vertical angle?

**Solution:**

Using the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Vertical Angle} &= 90^\circ - \text{Zenith Angle}\\
&= 90^\circ - 27^\circ\\
&= 63^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the vertical angle is $63^\circ$.

### Common Pitfalls
---------------------

*   **Forgetting to calculate the vertical angle correctly.**

    Make sure to use the formula: $\text{Vertical Angle} = 90^\circ - \text{Zenith Angle}$.
