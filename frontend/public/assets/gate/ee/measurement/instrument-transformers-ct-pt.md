**Instrument Transformers (CT/PT)**
=====================================

### Introduction
---------------

Instrument transformers are used to reduce the high voltage and current levels of electrical power systems to a level that can be measured by instruments. Current transformers (CTs) and potential transformers (PTs) are two types of instrument transformers commonly used in power systems.

### Core Concepts
-----------------

#### Current Transformers (CTs)

*   CTs are designed to measure the current flowing through a circuit.
*   They have a primary winding connected in series with the circuit and a secondary winding that is isolated from the primary.
*   The ratio of primary current to secondary current is known as the transformation ratio or turns ratio.

#### Potential Transformers (PTs)

*   PTs are designed to measure the voltage across a circuit.
*   They have a primary winding connected in parallel with the circuit and a secondary winding that is isolated from the primary.
*   The ratio of primary voltage to secondary voltage is known as the transformation ratio or turns ratio.

### Key Formulas/Theorems
-------------------------

$$\text{Transformation Ratio} = \frac{\text{Secondary Current (or Voltage)}}{\text{Primary Current (or Voltage)}}$$

### Problem Solving Patterns
-----------------------------

*   Identify the type of instrument transformer (CT or PT) and its application.
*   Determine the transformation ratio required for the measurement.
*   Calculate the secondary current or voltage using the transformation ratio.

### Examples with Solutions
---------------------------

**Example 1:**

A CT has a primary current of 100 A and a secondary current of 5 A. What is the transformation ratio?

$$\text{Transformation Ratio} = \frac{\text{Secondary Current}}{\text{Primary Current}} = \frac{5}{100} = 0.05$$

**Example 2:**

A PT has a primary voltage of 11 kV and a secondary voltage of 110 V. What is the transformation ratio?

$$\text{Transformation Ratio} = \frac{\text{Secondary Voltage}}{\text{Primary Voltage}} = \frac{110}{11,000} = 0.01$$

### Common Pitfalls
-------------------

*   Confusing the primary and secondary windings of a CT or PT.
*   Failing to calculate the transformation ratio correctly.

### Quick Summary
-----------------

*   Current transformers (CTs) measure current and have a primary winding connected in series with the circuit.
*   Potential transformers (PTs) measure voltage and have a primary winding connected in parallel with the circuit.
*   The transformation ratio is calculated as the secondary current or voltage divided by the primary current or voltage.
