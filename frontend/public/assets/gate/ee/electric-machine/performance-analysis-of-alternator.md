**Performance Analysis of Alternator**
=====================================

**Introduction**
---------------

The performance analysis of an alternator involves evaluating its ability to deliver electrical power under various operating conditions. This includes analyzing the relationship between the alternator's terminal voltage, field current, and armature current.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### Synchronous Impedance

Synchronous impedance is a measure of the opposition offered by the alternator to the flow of electrical current. It is defined as the ratio of the induced electromotive force (EMF) to the armature current.

$Z_s = \frac{E}{I_a}$

where $E$ is the induced EMF and $I_a$ is the armature current.

### Unsaturated and Saturated Synchronous Impedances

The unsaturated synchronous impedance, denoted as $(unsat)$, is the impedance of the alternator when it is operating under unsaturated conditions. The saturated synchronous impedance, denoted as $(sat)$, is the impedance of the alternator when it is operating under saturated conditions.

$Z_{unsat} = \frac{E}{I_a}$ (under unsaturated conditions)

$Z_{sat} = \frac{E}{I_a}$ (under saturated conditions)

### Short Circuit Characteristics

The short circuit characteristics of an alternator are represented by the short circuit current ($I_sc$) and the corresponding field current ($f I$).

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

*   The synchronous impedance is given by:

$Z_s = \frac{E}{I_a}$
*   The ratio of unsaturated to saturated synchronous impedances is given by:

$\frac{(unsat)}{(sat)} Z_s/Z_s = \frac{Z_{unsat}}{Z_{sat}}$

**Problem Solving Patterns**
-----------------------------

1.  **Identify the Alternator Type**: Determine if the alternator is delta-connected or star-connected.
2.  **Read the Graphs**: Carefully read the Open Circuit Characteristics (OCC) and Short Circuit Characteristics (SCC) graphs to identify the corresponding coordinates $(x, y)$.
3.  **Calculate Synchronous Impedance**: Use the OCC graph to calculate the synchronous impedance ($Z_s$).
4.  **Determine Unsaturated and Saturated Synchronous Impedances**: Calculate the ratio of unsaturated to saturated synchronous impedances using the SCC graph.

**Examples with Solutions**
---------------------------

### Example 1

Q: The figure below shows the per-phase OCC and SCC of a 14 kVA, 400 V, 50 Hz, 4-pole, 3-phase, delta connected alternator. Readings taken are marked as respective $(x,y)$ coordinates in the figure. Ratio of the unsaturated and saturated synchronous impedances $(unsat)/(sat) (Z_s/Z_s)$ of the alternator is closest to

A:

| Coordinate | OCC | SCC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $(8, 400)$ | $2,210$ | $1,110$ |
| $(0, 10)$ | $4,20$ | $0, 0$ |

Step 1: Identify the alternator type and read the graphs.

Alternator is delta-connected. From OCC graph, we can identify the following points:

*   Point A corresponds to a terminal voltage of 2,210 V and field current of 1,110 A.
*   Point B corresponds to a terminal voltage of 4,20 V and field current of 0, 0 A.

Step 2: Calculate synchronous impedance using OCC graph.

$Z_s = \frac{E}{I_a} = \frac{2200}{1110} = 1.98$

Step 3: Determine unsaturated and saturated synchronous impedances.

$\frac{(unsat)}{(sat)} Z_s/Z_s = \frac{2,20}{4,20} = 1.9$

**Common Pitfalls**
------------------

*   **Mistaking the Alternator Type**: Ensure that you correctly identify whether the alternator is delta-connected or star-connected.
*   **Incorrect Coordinate Reading**: Double-check your readings from the OCC and SCC graphs to avoid errors.
