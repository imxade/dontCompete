# Single Phase Induction Motor
## Introduction

A single phase induction motor (SPIM) is a type of AC motor that uses a single-phase power supply to produce rotation. It operates on the principle of electromagnetic induction, where a rotating magnetic field is created in the stator and induces currents in the rotor.

## Core Concepts

### Principle of Operation

The SPIM consists of two main parts: the stator and the rotor. The stator has a cylindrical shape with slots on its inner surface, which are filled with windings that carry an AC current. When an AC voltage is applied to the stator windings, a magnetic field is generated in the air gap between the stator and the rotor.

The rotor is usually a cylindrical object made of a ferromagnetic material, such as iron or steel, with slots on its surface. As the stator's magnetic field rotates, it induces an electromotive force (EMF) in the rotor windings, causing currents to flow in the rotor. These currents produce their own magnetic field, which interacts with the stator's magnetic field to create a torque.

### Types of Rotor Currents

There are two types of currents induced in the rotor:

* **Forward Field**: The forward field is the rotating magnetic field that is generated by the stator windings and induces currents in the rotor windings.
* **Backward Field**: The backward field is the rotating magnetic field that is generated by the rotor currents themselves, which interacts with the stator's magnetic field to create a torque.

## Key Formulas/Theorems

$$\omega_s = \frac{2\pi f}{P}$$
where $\omega_s$ is the synchronous speed of the motor (in rad/s), $f$ is the frequency of the power supply, and $P$ is the number of poles in the stator.

$$N_r = \frac{120f}{P}$$
where $N_r$ is the rotor speed (in rpm).

## Examples with Solutions

### Example 1: Synchronous Speed

Given:
$P = 10$, $f = 50 \text{ Hz}$

Find: $\omega_s$

Solution:

$$\omega_s = \frac{2\pi f}{P} = \frac{2\pi (50)}{10} = 31.416 \text{ rad/s}$$

### Example 2: Rotor Speed

Given:
$N_r = 540 \text{ rpm}$, $f = 50 \text{ Hz}$

Find: $P$

Solution:

$$N_r = \frac{120f}{P} \Rightarrow P = \frac{120(50)}{540} = 11.11 \text{ poles}$$

## Common Pitfalls

* Not accounting for the type of rotor current (forward or backward) when analyzing motor performance.
* Misusing formulas related to synchronous speed and rotor speed.
