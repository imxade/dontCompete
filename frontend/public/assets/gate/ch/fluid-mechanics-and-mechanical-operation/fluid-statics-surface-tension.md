**Fluid Statics and Surface Tension**
=====================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Fluid statics and surface tension are fundamental concepts in fluid mechanics that describe the behavior of fluids at rest or under equilibrium conditions. These phenomena play a crucial role in various engineering applications, such as design of ships, offshore structures, and chemical processing equipment.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### Fluid Statics

*   A fluid is considered to be in a state of static equilibrium when its particles are at rest.
*   The pressure exerted by a fluid on an object submerged in it is given by $P = P_0 + \rho g h$, where:
    *   $P$ is the absolute pressure,
    *   $P_0$ is the atmospheric pressure,
    *   $\rho$ is the fluid density,
    *   $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and
    *   $h$ is the height of the fluid above the reference level.

### Surface Tension

*   Surface tension is a property of fluids that arises from intermolecular forces between molecules at the surface.
*   It acts as an "elastic skin" on the surface of the liquid, causing it to behave like a stretched membrane.
*   The surface tension ($\sigma$) can be measured experimentally using techniques such as the capillary rise method or the Wilhelmy plate method.

### Key Formulas/Theorems

$$
P = P_0 + \rho g h
$$

$$
\sigma = \frac{F}{l}
$$

where:

*   $F$ is the force acting on the surface, and
*   $l$ is the length of the surface.

---------------------------

3.  Choose the appropriate formula or theorem based on the type of problem and the given data.
4.  Plug in values and perform calculations carefully.

**Examples with Solutions**
-------------------------

### Example 1: Pressure at a Depth

A tank contains water with a depth of 10 m. Calculate the pressure at a point 5 m below the surface, given that $\rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$ and $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$.

Solution:

$$
P = P_0 + \rho g h
$$

$$
P = 1.01 \times 10^5 + (1000 \times 9.81 \times 5)
$$

$$
P \approx 1.20 \times 10^5 \text{ Pa}
$$

### Example 2: Force due to Surface Tension

A soap film of surface tension $\sigma = 30 \text{ mN/m}$ is stretched between two pins 50 cm apart. Calculate the force exerted by the film on each pin.

Solution:

$$
\sigma = \frac{F}{l}
$$

$$
F = \sigma l
$$

$$
F = (30 \times 10^{-3}) \times (0.5)
$$

$$
F = 15 \text{ mN}
$$

**Common Pitfalls**
-------------------

When working with fluid statics and surface tension, be cautious of:

*   Incorrect unit conversions or assumptions about the system.
*   Failure to consider external factors such as gravity, container geometry, or temperature effects on density.

**Quick Summary**
-----------------

*   Fluid statics deals with pressure in fluids at rest.
*   Surface tension arises from intermolecular forces at fluid surfaces.
*   Key formulas include $P = P_0 + \rho g h$ and $\sigma = F/l$.
