**Laminar Flow Through a Capillary Tube**
======================================

### Introduction

Laminar flow through a capillary tube is an important concept in fluid mechanics, describing the flow of fluids through narrow tubes. This topic is crucial for understanding various industrial and engineering applications.

### Core Concepts

*   **Laminar Flow**: A type of fluid flow characterized by smooth, orderly layers (laminae) of fluid particles.
*   **Capillary Tube**: A tube with a small diameter, typically used to measure the viscosity of fluids.
*   **Hagen-Poiseuille Equation**: A mathematical formula describing the relationship between pressure drop, flow rate, and viscosity in laminar flow.

### Key Formulas/Theorems

$$\Delta P = \frac{8 \mu L Q}{\pi r^4}$$

where:

*   $\Delta P$ is the pressure drop across the tube
*   $Q$ is the flow rate of the fluid
*   $r$ is the radius of the tube
*   $L$ is the length of the tube
*   $\mu$ is the dynamic viscosity of the fluid

### Problem Solving Patterns

1.  **Identify the type of flow**: Determine if the flow is laminar or turbulent.
2.  **Apply the Hagen-Poiseuille Equation**: Use the formula to relate pressure drop, flow rate, and viscosity.
3.  **Solve for unknown variables**: Rearrange the equation to isolate the variable of interest (e.g., $\mu$).

### Examples with Solutions

**Example 1**

A capillary tube has a diameter of $0.5$ mm and a length of $1.5$ m. The fluid flow is laminar, steady, and fully developed. For a flow rate of $3 \times 10^{-6}$ cm$^3$/s, the pressure drop across the length of the tube is $1$ MPa. If the viscosity of the fluid is $3 \times 10^{-2}$ Pa·s, what is the value of $\mu$?

**Solution**

1.  Convert the flow rate to SI units: $Q = 3 \times 10^{-6}$ cm$^3$/s $= 3 \times 10^{-9}$ m$^3$/s
2.  Calculate the radius of the tube: $r = \frac{d}{2} = \frac{0.5}{2} = 0.25$ mm $= 0.00025$ m
3.  Apply the Hagen-Poiseuille Equation:

$$\Delta P = \frac{8 \mu L Q}{\pi r^4}$$

Rearrange to solve for $\mu$:

$$\mu = \frac{\Delta P \pi r^4}{8 L Q}$$

Substitute values:

$$\mu = \frac{1 \times 10^6 \times \pi \times (0.00025)^4}{8 \times 1.5 \times 3 \times 10^{-9}}$$

Simplify and calculate $\mu$.

### Common Pitfalls

*   **Incorrectly identifying the type of flow**: Ensure that the flow is indeed laminar.
*   **Misapplying the Hagen-Poiseuille Equation**: Double-check units and dimensions.
