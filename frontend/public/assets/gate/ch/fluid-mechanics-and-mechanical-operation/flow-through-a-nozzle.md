**Flow Through a Nozzle**
=========================

**Introduction**
---------------

The flow through a nozzle is an important concept in fluid mechanics, which deals with the behavior of fluids under various conditions. A nozzle is a device that increases the velocity of a fluid by reducing its cross-sectional area. This leads to a decrease in pressure and an increase in kinetic energy.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### Bernoulli's Equation

The fundamental principle governing flow through a nozzle is Bernoulli's equation, which states that:

$$P + \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant}$$

where $P$ is the pressure, $\rho$ is the density of the fluid, $v$ is the velocity, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $h$ is the height above a reference point.

### Continuity Equation

The continuity equation states that:

$$\frac{dA}{dt} = \text{mass flow rate} = \rho A v$$

where $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the nozzle.

**Examples with Solutions**
-------------------------

### Example 1: Flow Through a Nozzle (GATE 2023)

A cylindrical tank with a diameter of 500 mm contains water up to a height $h$. A 5 mm diameter round nozzle, whose center is 1 cm above the base of the tank, has its exit open to the atmosphere as shown in the schematic below. The pressure above the water level in the tank is maintained at 2 bar (absolute). Neglect all frictional and entry/exit losses. Use acceleration due to gravity as $10 m/s^2$ and atmospheric pressure as 1 bar.

The absolute value of initial $\frac{dh}{dt}$ when $h = 51 cm$ is equal to ________ (rounded off to two decimal places).

**Solution**

Applying Bernoulli's equation point $(i)$ and $(ii)$:

$$P + \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 + \rho g h = P' + \frac{1}{2} \rho v'^2 + \rho g h'$$

where $P$ is the pressure at point $(i)$, $v$ is the velocity at point $(i)$, $h$ is the height above a reference point, and $P'$ is the pressure at point $(ii)$.

Simplifying and solving for $\frac{dh}{dt}$:

$$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{1}{g} \left( \frac{v'^2 - v^2}{2h'} \right)$$

Substituting values, we get:

$$\frac{dh}{dt} = 0.14 m/s$$

**Common Pitfalls**
-------------------

When solving problems involving flow through a nozzle, students often miss the following points:

*   Failing to apply Bernoulli's equation correctly.
*   Not considering frictional and entry/exit losses.
*   Ignoring the continuity equation.
