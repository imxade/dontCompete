# Fluid Statics, Surface Tension, Newtonian and Non-Newtonian Fluids, Transport Properties

## Introduction
Fluid mechanics is a branch of physics that deals with the behavior of fluids under various conditions. This topic covers static fluids (in equilibrium), surface tension, Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids, and transport properties.

## Core Concepts

### Fluid Statics

* **Pressure**: The force exerted by a fluid on an object or surface due to its weight.
* **Hydrostatic Pressure Equation**:
\[ P = \rho g h + P_0 \]
where $\rho$ is the density of the fluid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, $h$ is the depth of the fluid column, and $P_0$ is the atmospheric pressure.
* **Gauge Pressure**: The pressure above or below atmospheric pressure.

### Surface Tension

* **Surface Tension**: A measure of the cohesive energy at a liquid's surface. It acts as a "skin" on the liquid's surface.
* **Surface Tension Equation**:
\[ \gamma = \frac{\text{Energy per unit area}}{\text{Length}} \]
where $\gamma$ is the surface tension.

### Newtonian and Non-Newtonian Fluids

* **Newtonian Fluid**: A fluid whose viscosity is constant and independent of shear rate.
* **Non-Newtonian Fluid**: A fluid whose viscosity depends on shear rate or other factors (e.g., temperature, pressure).
* **Shear Stress**:
\[ \tau = -\mu \frac{dv}{dy} \]
where $\tau$ is the shear stress, $\mu$ is the dynamic viscosity, and $dv/dy$ is the velocity gradient.

### Transport Properties

* **Viscosity**: A measure of a fluid's resistance to flow.
* **Dynamic Viscosity**:
\[ \mu = \frac{\text{Shear Stress}}{\text{Velocity Gradient}} \]
where $\mu$ is the dynamic viscosity, and $dv/dy$ is the velocity gradient.

## Key Formulas/Theorems

\[ P = \rho g h + P_0 \]
\[ \gamma = \frac{\text{Energy per unit area}}{\text{Length}} \]
\[ \tau = -\mu \frac{dv}{dy} \]
\[ \mu = \frac{\text{Shear Stress}}{\text{Velocity Gradient}} \]

## Problem Solving Patterns

* For fluid statics, use the hydrostatic pressure equation to find pressures and forces.
* For surface tension, consider the energy balance across the liquid's surface.
* For Newtonian fluids, apply the shear stress equation and assume constant viscosity.
* For non-Newtonian fluids, consider the specific type of fluid (e.g., Bingham plastic) and its behavior.

## Examples with Solutions

### Example 1: Fluid Statics
A liquid has a density of $1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$ and an atmospheric pressure of $10^5 \text{ Pa}$. Find the hydrostatic pressure at a depth of $20 \text{ m}$.
\[ P = (1000)(9.8)(20) + 10^5 = 2.96 \times 10^6 \text{ Pa} \]

### Example 2: Surface Tension
A liquid has a surface tension of $72 \text{ mN/m}$. Find the energy per unit area if the length is $1 \text{ cm}$.
\[ \gamma = (72)(0.01) = 7.2 \times 10^{-3} \text{ J/m}^2 \]

### Example 3: Newtonian Fluids
A liquid has a dynamic viscosity of $0.5 \text{ Pa s}$. Find the shear stress if the velocity gradient is $20 \text{ m/s}$.
\[ \tau = (-)(0.5)(20) = -10 \text{ Pa} \]

### Example 4: Non-Newtonian Fluids
A Bingham plastic has a yield stress of $1000 \text{ Pa}$ and a dynamic viscosity of $200 \text{ Pa s}$. Find the shear stress if the velocity gradient is $50 \text{ m/s}$.
\[ \tau = (1000) + (200)(50) = 13000 \text{ Pa} \]

## Common Pitfalls

* For fluid statics, forget to include atmospheric pressure in calculations.
* For surface tension, neglect the energy balance across the liquid's surface.
* For Newtonian fluids, assume non-constant viscosity.
* For non-Newtonian fluids, choose an incorrect model or parameters.

## Quick Summary

* Fluid statics: Use hydrostatic pressure equation for pressures and forces.
* Surface tension: Consider energy balance across liquid's surface.
* Newtonian fluids: Apply shear stress equation with constant viscosity.
* Non-Newtonian fluids: Choose correct model and parameters for specific fluid type.
