**Physical Constants and Units**
=====================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Physical constants are fundamental quantities that do not change with time or location, while units are standardized measures of physical quantities. Understanding these concepts is crucial for accurate calculations and problem-solving in various fields, including engineering.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

* **SI Units**: The International System of Units (SI) provides a coherent and consistent framework for measuring physical quantities.
* **Physical Constants**: These constants represent fundamental properties of the universe, such as the speed of light (c), Planck's constant (h), and the gravitational constant (G).
* **Units Conversion**: Accurate unit conversion is essential to avoid errors in calculations.

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
------------------------

* **Speed of Light**: $c = 299,792,458 \text{ m/s}$
* **Planck's Constant**: $h = 6.62607015 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J s}$
* **Gravitational Constant**: $G = 6.67430(81) \times 10^{-11} \text{ N m^2 kg^-2}$

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

### Thermal Conductivity

* Match the correct unit with thermal conductivity: $\text{W/m K}$
* Use the formula: $Q = kA\frac{\Delta T}{\Delta x}$

### Convective Heat Transfer Coefficient

* Match the correct unit with convective heat transfer coefficient: $\text{W/m^2 K}$
* Use the formula: $h = \frac{k}{x}$ or $h = \frac{Q}{A\Delta T}$

### Stefan-Boltzmann Constant

* Match the correct unit with Stefan-Boltzmann constant: $\text{W/m^2 K^4}$
* Use the formula: $\sigma = \frac{\pi^5 k^4}{60 h^3 c^2}$

### Heat Capacity Rate

* Match the correct unit with heat capacity rate: $\text{J/K}$ or $\text{W/K}$
* Use the formula: $C = \frac{Q}{\Delta T}$

**Examples with Solutions**
-------------------------

1. A rod of length 0.5 m and radius 0.01 m has a temperature difference of 20 K between its ends. If it takes 2 hours to cool down, what is the thermal conductivity of the material?

   \begin{align*}
   k &= \frac{Q}{A\Delta T}\cdot t\\
   Q &= \text{energy transferred}\\
   A &= \text{area of rod}\\
   t &= \text{time taken}\\
   \end{align*}

   We can solve for $k$ by rearranging the formula: $k = \frac{Q}{A\Delta T}\cdot t$

2. A heat exchanger has a surface area of 5 m^2 and convective heat transfer coefficient of 100 W/m^2 K. If the temperature difference between the two fluids is 50 K, what is the rate of heat transfer?

   \begin{align*}
   Q &= hA\Delta T\\
   A &= \text{surface area}\\
   h &= \text{convective heat transfer coefficient}\\
   T &= \text{temperature difference}\\
   \end{align*}

   We can solve for $Q$ by plugging in the given values: $Q = (100 \text{ W/m^2 K})(5 \text{ m}^2)(50 \text{ K})$

**Common Pitfalls**
