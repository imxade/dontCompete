**Thermodynamics for Chemical Engineering**
=============================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Thermodynamics is a branch of physical science that deals with heat, work, and temperature. It provides a framework to understand and predict the behavior of thermodynamic systems, which are crucial in chemical engineering. This note will cover the fundamental concepts, laws, and formulas required for thermodynamic calculations.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### 1. **Thermodynamic System**

A thermodynamic system is defined as a region of space where matter and energy can be exchanged with its surroundings. The system's boundaries are determined by the observer, and it may be isolated, closed, or open.

### 2. **Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics**

The zeroth law states that if two systems are in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are also in thermal equilibrium with each other.

### 3. **First Law of Thermodynamics (Conservation of Energy)**

The first law states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another:

$$\Delta E = Q - W$$

where $\Delta E$ is the change in internal energy, $Q$ is the heat added to the system, and $W$ is the work done by the system.

### 4. **Second Law of Thermodynamics (Entropy)**

The second law states that the total entropy of an isolated system always increases over time:

$$\Delta S \geq \frac{Q}{T}$$

where $\Delta S$ is the change in entropy, $Q$ is the heat added to the system, and $T$ is the temperature at which the heat is added.

### 5. **Ideal Gas Law**

The ideal gas law relates the pressure, volume, and temperature of an ideal gas:

$$PV = nRT$$

where $P$ is the pressure, $V$ is the volume, $n$ is the number of moles, $R$ is the gas constant, and $T$ is the temperature.

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

### 1. **Internal Energy**

The internal energy of an ideal gas is a function of temperature only:

$$U = \frac{3}{2}nRT$$

for a monatomic ideal gas.

### 2. **Heat Capacity**

The heat capacity of an ideal gas is defined as the amount of heat required to raise its temperature by one degree:

$$C_V = \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta T}$$

where $C_V$ is the heat capacity at constant volume, $\Delta U$ is the change in internal energy, and $\Delta T$ is the change in temperature.

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

1.  **Work Done by a Spring**: When a spring exerts a force on an object, the work done can be calculated using the formula:

    $$W = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$$

    where $W$ is the work done, $k$ is the spring constant, and $x$ is the displacement.
2.  **Heat Transfer**: When heat is transferred between two systems, the amount of heat transferred can be calculated using the formula:

    $$Q = mc\Delta T$$

    where $Q$ is the heat transferred, $m$ is the mass of the system, $c$ is the specific heat capacity, and $\Delta T$ is the change in temperature.

**Examples with Solutions**
---------------------------

### 1. **Example: Work Done by a Spring**

A spring with a force constant of 0.3 N/m is compressed by 0.2 m. Calculate the work done:

```latex
W = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}(0.3)(0.2)^2 = 0.006 J
```

### 2. **Example: Heat Transfer**

A system with a mass of 0.5 kg is heated from 300 K to 400 K. Calculate the heat transferred if the specific heat capacity is 1000 J/kg·K:

```latex
Q = mc\Delta T = (0.5)(1000)(400 - 300) = 25000 J
```

**Common Pitfalls**
-----------------

1.  **Forgetting to account for the work done by a spring**: When calculating the final equilibrium temperature, don't forget to include the work done by the spring.
2.  **Not using the correct formula for heat capacity**: Make sure to use the correct formula for heat capacity, which depends on whether the system is at constant volume or constant pressure.

**Quick Summary**
----------------

*   Thermodynamics deals with heat, work, and temperature.
*   The zeroth law states that if two systems are in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are also in thermal equilibrium with each other.
*   The first law states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another.
*   The second law states that the total entropy of an isolated system always increases over time.
*   The ideal gas law relates the pressure, volume, and temperature of an ideal gas.
