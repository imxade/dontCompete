**Thermodynamics: Process Calculations and Thermodynamic Relations**
===========================================================

### Introduction

Thermodynamics deals with the relationships between heat, work, and energy. It provides a framework for analyzing and predicting the behavior of systems under various conditions. The van der Waals equation of state and the concept of compressibility factor are crucial in understanding thermodynamic processes.

### Core Concepts

#### 1. Van der Waals Equation of State

The van der Waals equation is an extension of the ideal gas law, accounting for the attractive and repulsive forces between molecules:

\[ \left( P + \frac{a}{v^2} \right) (v - b) = RT \]

where:
- $P$ is the pressure
- $v$ is the molar volume
- $R$ is the gas constant
- $T$ is the temperature
- $a$ and $b$ are constants that depend on the gas

The compressibility factor, $z$, is defined as:

\[ z = \frac{P v}{RT} \]

At the critical point, $z_c = 3/8$.

#### 2. Thermodynamic Relations

The first law of thermodynamics relates the change in internal energy ($\Delta U$) to the heat added ($Q$) and work done ($W$):

\[ \Delta U = Q - W \]

For an ideal gas, the molar internal energy is a function of temperature only:

\[ u(T) = C_v T \]

where $C_v$ is the specific heat capacity at constant volume.

### Key Formulas/Theorems

*   Van der Waals equation: $\left( P + \frac{a}{v^2} \right) (v - b) = RT$
*   Compressibility factor: $z = \frac{P v}{RT}$
*   Molar internal energy for an ideal gas: $u(T) = C_v T$

### Problem Solving Patterns

1.  **Van der Waals Equation:** When applying the van der Waals equation, ensure to identify the constants $a$ and $b$, and use them correctly in the calculation.
2.  **Compressibility Factor:** In problems involving compressibility factor, focus on understanding the conditions under which it is defined (e.g., critical point).

### Examples with Solutions

**Q1: Compressibility Factor at Critical Point**

*   Given: $z_c = \frac{3}{8}$
*   We are asked to find the value of $z$ when $\frac{r v}{3} = 0.75$ and $\frac{r T}{4} = 1.25$

## Step-by-step solution

1.  Calculate reduced temperature, $T_r$, from given data.
2.  Use the van der Waals equation to express $P$ in terms of $v$, $R$, and $T_r$.
3.  Express compressibility factor, $z$, using the calculated $P$.

**Q2: Final Pressure in a Closed Vessel**

*   Given: Initial conditions (temperature, pressure, moles) for CO, O$_2$, and N$_2$
*   We are asked to find the final pressure after combustion

## Step-by-step solution

1.  Write balanced chemical equations for CO and O$_2$ reactions.
2.  Calculate heat of reaction using $\Delta H_r = -\Delta H_f^{\circ}$
3.  Use ideal gas law to relate initial and final conditions.

### Common Pitfalls

*   Misapplication of the van der Waals equation or compressibility factor
*   Incorrect handling of units or variables in thermodynamic equations
*   Failure to account for temperature dependence in specific heat capacities
