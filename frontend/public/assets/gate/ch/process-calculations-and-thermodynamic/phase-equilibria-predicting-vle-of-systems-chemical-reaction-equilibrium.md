**Phase Equilibria Predicting VLE of Systems Chemical Reaction Equilibrium**
====================================================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Phase equilibria predict the behavior of systems at equilibrium, including vapor-liquid equilibria (VLE). In chemical reaction equilibrium, we analyze the concentrations of reactants and products to determine if a system is in equilibrium. This concept is crucial in process calculations and thermodynamics.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### Phase Equilibria

Phase equilibria occur when two or more phases coexist in a system at equilibrium. The most common types are:

*   **Vapor-Liquid Equilibrium (VLE)**: A mixture of vapor and liquid phases.
*   **Liquid-Liquid Equilibrium (LLE)**: Two immiscible liquids.

### Phase Rule

The phase rule, also known as Gibbs' phase rule, relates the number of components (C), phases (P), and degrees of freedom (F):

```latex
F = C - P + 2
```

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
------------------------

*   **Raoult's Law**: For a binary solution, the vapor pressure is given by:

$$P_{total} = x_1P^{sat}_1 + x_2P^{sat}_2$$

where $x_i$ is the mole fraction of component i.
*   **Henry's Law**: For a dilute solution, the partial pressure of a gas is proportional to its concentration:

$$P_i = kH \cdot C_i$$

where $kH$ is Henry's constant.

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

1.  Identify the type of phase equilibrium (VLE or LLE).
2.  Determine the number of components and phases.
3.  Apply the phase rule to find degrees of freedom.
4.  Use Raoult's Law for VLE or Henry's Law for dilute solutions.

**Examples with Solutions**
---------------------------

### Example 1: VLE

A binary solution of ethanol (E) and water (W) is at equilibrium. The vapor pressure of pure E is 100 kPa, and the mole fraction of W in the liquid phase is 0.2. Find the partial pressure of E.

```latex
P_E = x_1 \cdot P^{sat}_E + x_2 \cdot P^{sat}_W
= 0.8 \cdot 100\, kPa + 0.2 \cdot 0\, kPa
= 80\, kPa
```

### Example 2: Chemical Reaction Equilibrium

A reaction between A and B forms product C:

$$A + B \rightleftharpoons C$$

The equilibrium constant (K) is given by:

```latex
K = \frac{[C]}{[A][B]}
```

If the initial concentrations are [A] = 0.1 M, [B] = 0.2 M, and K = 10, find the equilibrium concentration of C.

Let $x$ be the change in concentration:

$$[A]_{eq} = 0.1 - x$$

$$[B]_{eq} = 0.2 - x$$

$$[C]_{eq} = x$$

Substitute into the equilibrium expression:

```latex
10 = \frac{x}{(0.1-x)(0.2-x)}
```

Solve for $x$ (equilibrium concentration of C).

**Common Pitfalls**
--------------------

*   Failing to identify the type of phase equilibrium.
*   Misapplying Raoult's Law or Henry's Law.
