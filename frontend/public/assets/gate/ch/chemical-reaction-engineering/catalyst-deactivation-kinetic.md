**Catalyst Deactivation Kinetics**
=====================================

### Introduction

Catalyst deactivation refers to the decrease in catalytic activity over time, leading to a reduction in reaction rates and yields. Understanding catalyst deactivation kinetics is crucial in chemical reaction engineering as it helps design more efficient reactors and optimize catalyst usage.

### Core Concepts

**Instantaneous Activity**

The instantaneous activity of a catalyst at time `t` is defined as the ratio of the rate of reaction with the used catalyst to the rate of reaction with fresh catalyst:

$$a_t = \frac{r'(t)}{R_0}$$

where $r'(t)$ is the rate of reaction at time `t`, and $R_0$ is the initial rate of reaction.

**Empirical Correlation**

The given empirical correlation for the instantaneous activity is:

$$\ln(r'(t)) = -kt + C$$

However, in this case, we are provided with an empirical correlation that relates the rate of reaction to time `t`:

$$r_t(t) = \frac{A}{1 + (t/10)^0.5}$$

where $A$ is a constant.

### Key Formulas/Theorems

* **Instantaneous Activity Formula**: $a_t = \frac{r'(t)}{R_0}$
* **Empirical Correlation**: $\ln(r'(t)) = -kt + C$
