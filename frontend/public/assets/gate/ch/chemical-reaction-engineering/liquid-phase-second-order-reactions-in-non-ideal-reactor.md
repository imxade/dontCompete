**Liquid Phase Second Order Reactions in Non-Ideal Reactors**
===========================================================

**Introduction**
---------------

In this section, we will cover the key concepts and formulas required to tackle problems related to liquid phase second order reactions in non-ideal reactors. This is a crucial topic in chemical reaction engineering, particularly for students of GATE (Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering) Chemical Engineering.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### Irreversible Liquid Phase Second Order Reaction

The given reaction is:

$$A + B \rightarrow$$ with rate constant $k = 0.2 \text{ L/mol/min}$.

This is an irreversible second-order reaction, where the concentration of the reactants affects the reaction rate.

### Non-Ideal Reactor

In a non-ideal reactor, the residence time distribution (RTD) curve is not a perfect plug flow. Instead, it follows a specific pattern, which can be represented by the $E-t$ curve.

### Segregated Model

The segregated model is used to mimic the non-ideality of this reactor. It assumes that the tracer and reactants are perfectly mixed at all times.

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

$$\int_{0}^{\infty} E(t) dt = 1$$

This formula represents the area under the $E-t$ curve, which is equal to 1 for a non-ideal reactor.

### Conversion of Reactant A

The conversion of reactant $A$ can be calculated using the following formula:

$$X_{A} = \frac{K C}{t}$$

where $K$ is the reaction rate constant, $C$ is the concentration of reactant $A$, and $t$ is time.

### Equilibrium Conversion

At equilibrium, the conversion of reactant $A$ can be calculated using:

$$X_{A} = \frac{1}{2 K C t + 1}$$

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

### Approach 1: Given Data and Formula Application

When given data on rate constants, concentrations, and times, students should apply the formulas above to calculate the conversion of reactant $A$.

### Example with Solution

Given:
$k = 0.2 \text{ L/mol/min}$,
$C_{A} = 1.5 \text{ mol/L}$,
$t = 15 \text{ min}$,

Calculate the conversion of reactant $A$:

$$X_{A} = \frac{K C}{t} = \frac{(0.2) (1.5)}{15} = 0.02$$

**Common Pitfalls**
-------------------

* Failing to recognize non-ideal reactor conditions.
* Incorrect application of formulas.
