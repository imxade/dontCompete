**Atomic Radius and Density Calculation**
=====================================

**Introduction**
---------------

In material science, atomic radius and density are crucial parameters for understanding the properties of materials. The atomic radius is a fundamental property that affects various physical and chemical processes, while density provides valuable information about the compactness and weight of a substance.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

*   **Atomic Radius**: The distance between the nucleus of an atom and its outermost electron in a neutral atom.
*   **Density**: Mass per unit volume of a substance. It is expressed in units of kg/m³ (kilograms per cubic meter).
*   **Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) Structure**: A crystal structure where each unit cell has one atom at the center and eight atoms at the corners.

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

The atomic radius can be calculated using the following formulas:

1.  **Atomic Radius (r)**: $r = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{2}}$ for a Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) structure, where $a$ is the lattice parameter.
2.  **Density ($\rho$)**: $\rho = \frac{nM}{N_A}$, where:
    *   $n$ is the number of atoms per unit cell,
    *   $M$ is the molar mass of the substance,
    *   $N_A$ is Avogadro's number.

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

To solve problems involving atomic radius and density, follow these steps:

1.  **Determine the crystal structure**: Identify whether the material has an FCC, Body-Centered Cubic (BCC), or Hexagonal Close-Packed (HCP) structure.
2.  **Calculate the atomic radius**: Use the appropriate formula for the given structure.
3.  **Find the lattice parameter ($a$)**: Look up the value in a reliable source or use another method to calculate it.
4.  **Determine the number of atoms per unit cell (n)**: This depends on the crystal structure.
5.  **Calculate the density**: Use the formula for density, plugging in the values for $M$, $N_A$, and $n$.

**Examples with Solutions**
---------------------------

### Example 1:

A hypothetical FCC metal has an atomic radius of $2.10 \times 10^{-10}$ m. The atomic weight is 24.092 g/mol. Taking Avogadro's number to be $6.023 \times 10^{23}$ atoms/mol, calculate the density of the metal.

#### Solution:

1.  **Calculate the lattice parameter ($a$)**: Since it's an FCC structure, we can use the formula for atomic radius to find $a$: $r = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{2}}$. Rearrange to solve for $a$: $a = 2\sqrt{2} \times r = 2\sqrt{2} \times 2.10 \times 10^{-10}$ m.
2.  **Find the number of atoms per unit cell (n)**: For an FCC structure, $n = 4$.
3.  **Calculate the density**: Plug in the values for $M$, $N_A$, and $n$: $\rho = \frac{nM}{N_A} = \frac{4 \times 24.092 \text{ g/mol}}{6.023 \times 10^{23} \text{ atoms/mol}}$.

### Example 2:

A metal has a density of 9.0 g/cm³. If the atomic weight is 55.847 g/mol, and Avogadro's number is $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ atoms/mol, calculate the atomic radius.

#### Solution:

1.  **Rearrange the formula for density**: $\rho = \frac{nM}{N_A}$. We can rearrange this to solve for $n$: $n = \frac{\rho N_A}{M}$.
2.  **Plug in the values**: Use the given values for $\rho$, $M$, and $N_A$ to find $n$.
3.  **Calculate the lattice parameter ($a$)**: Once you have $n$, use it to find $a$: $r = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{2}}$. Solve for $a$: $a = 2\sqrt{2} \times r = 2\sqrt{2} \times \text{atomic radius}$.

**Common Pitfalls**
-------------------

*   **Mixing up the crystal structures**: Make sure to use the correct formula and value for the given structure.
*   **Rounding errors**: Be careful when rounding numbers, especially in calculations involving large values like Avogadro's number.
*   **Forgetting units**: Pay attention to units, as this can affect the accuracy of your calculations.

------------------

*   Density provides information about compactness and weight.
*   Use the correct formula for the crystal structure.
*   Pay attention to units and rounding errors.
