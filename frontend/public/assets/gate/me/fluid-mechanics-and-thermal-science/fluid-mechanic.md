**Fluid Mechanics and Thermal Science**
=====================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Fluid mechanics is a branch of physics that deals with the behavior of fluids, which are substances that flow freely. In this topic, we will cover various aspects of fluid mechanics, including pressure measurement, fluid dynamics, and hydraulic machinery.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### 1. Pressure Measurement

Pressure is defined as the normal force exerted per unit area on an object. The absolute pressure at a point in a fluid is given by:

$$P = P_{atm} + \rho g H$$

where $P$ is the absolute pressure, $P_{atm}$ is the atmospheric pressure, $\rho$ is the density of the fluid, $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity, and $H$ is the depth below the surface.

### 2. Fluid Dynamics

Fluid dynamics deals with the motion of fluids. The continuity equation states that the mass flow rate is constant throughout a system:

$$\dot{m} = \rho A v$$

where $\dot{m}$ is the mass flow rate, $\rho$ is the density of the fluid, $A$ is the cross-sectional area, and $v$ is the velocity.

The momentum equation states that the net force acting on a system is equal to the change in momentum:

$$\sum F = \frac{\partial}{\partial t} (\rho A v) + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} (A P)$$

### 3. Hydraulic Machinery

Hydraulic machinery deals with the use of fluids to transmit power. The hydraulic efficiency of a turbine is given by:

$$\eta_h = \frac{P_{out}}{P_{in}}$$

where $P_{out}$ is the output power and $P_{in}$ is the input power.

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

### 1. Pressure Measurement

*   Absolute pressure: $P = P_{atm} + \rho g H$
*   Gauge pressure: $P_g = P - P_{atm}$

### 2. Fluid Dynamics

*   Continuity equation: $\dot{m} = \rho A v$
*   Momentum equation: $\sum F = \frac{\partial}{\partial t} (\rho A v) + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} (A P)$

### 3. Hydraulic Machinery

*   Hydraulic efficiency: $\eta_h = \frac{P_{out}}{P_{in}}$

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

### 1. Pressure Measurement

*   Identify the atmospheric pressure and fluid density.
*   Use the absolute pressure formula to calculate the depth.

### 2. Fluid Dynamics

*   Identify the mass flow rate, cross-sectional area, and velocity.
*   Use the continuity equation to relate these quantities.

### 3. Hydraulic Machinery

*   Identify the output power and input power.
*   Use the hydraulic efficiency formula to calculate the efficiency.

**Examples with Solutions**
---------------------------

### Example 1: Pressure Measurement

A pressure measurement device reads an absolute pressure of $4.2 \times 10^6$ Pa. The atmospheric pressure is $101 \times 10^3$ Pa, and the density of sea water is $3 \times 10^3$ kg/m$^3$. Calculate the depth.

$$H = \frac{P - P_{atm}}{\rho g} = \frac{(4.2 \times 10^6 - 101 \times 10^3)}{(3 \times 10^3) (9.8)}$$

### Example 2: Fluid Dynamics

Water flows out from a tank with a cross-sectional area of $2 \times 1$ m$^2$. The orifice has a cross-sectional area of $2 \times 10^{-4}$ m$^2$, and the initial water level is $1$ m. Calculate the time taken for the water to reach a level of $4 H$.

$$\dot{m} = \rho A v \Rightarrow t = \frac{\Delta V}{\dot{m}}$$

### Example 3: Hydraulic Machinery

A vertical shaft Francis turbine has an available head of $200$ m and a tip speed of $40$ m/s. The velocity at the exit of the draft tube is $3.5$ m/s, and the flow rate is $20$ m$^3$/s. Calculate the hydraulic efficiency.

$$\eta_h = \frac{P_{out}}{P_{in}} \Rightarrow P_{out} = \rho g Q H_e$$

**Common Pitfalls**
------------------

*   Forgetting to convert units.
*   Not considering losses in hydraulic machinery.
*   Incorrectly applying the continuity equation.

**Quick Summary**
-----------------

Fluid mechanics deals with the behavior of fluids. Key concepts include pressure measurement, fluid dynamics, and hydraulic machinery. The continuity equation relates mass flow rate, cross-sectional area, and velocity. The momentum equation states that the net force is equal to the change in momentum. Hydraulic efficiency is given by the ratio of output power to input power.
