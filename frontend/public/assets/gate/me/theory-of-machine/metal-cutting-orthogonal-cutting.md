**Metal Cutting Orthogonal Cutting**
=====================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Orthogonal cutting is a fundamental process in metal cutting, where the cutting tool moves perpendicular to the workpiece's surface. This topic focuses on the principles and calculations involved in orthogonal cutting.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### 1. Cutting Forces

The cutting force (F_c) is the primary force acting on the cutting tool during orthogonal cutting. It can be calculated using the following formula:

$$ F_c = \frac{W}{2} \cdot t_s \cdot \tau $$

where:

* $W$ is the width of cut
* $t_s$ is the shear angle (also known as the shear plane angle)
* $\tau$ is the dynamic yield shear strength of the workpiece material

### 2. Friction Angle and Shear Angle

The friction angle ($\phi$) is the angle between the friction force and the normal force on the rake face. The shear angle ($\alpha_s$) is the angle between the shear plane and the normal to the cutting edge.

In orthogonal cutting, the relationship between the friction angle and the shear angle can be expressed as:

$$ \tan(\phi) = \frac{\cos(\rho)}{\sin(\alpha_s)} $$

where $\rho$ is the rake angle of the cutting tool.

### 3. Shear Plane Angle and Chip Formation

The shear plane angle ($\alpha_s$) determines the shape of the chip formed during orthogonal cutting. A smaller shear plane angle results in a thicker, more curved chip, while a larger shear angle produces a thinner, more straight chip.

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

### 1. Cutting Force Formula

$$ F_c = \frac{W}{2} \cdot t_s \cdot \tau $$

### 2. Friction Angle and Shear Angle Relationship

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

When solving problems related to orthogonal cutting, follow these steps:

1. Identify the given parameters: width of cut (W), shear angle ($\alpha_s$), dynamic yield shear strength of the workpiece material ($\tau$), and rake angle ($\rho$).
2. Calculate the friction angle using the relationship between $\phi$, $\rho$, and $\alpha_s$.
3. Apply the cutting force formula to determine $F_c$.

**Examples with Solutions**
---------------------------

### Example 1

A turning operation is performed on a metallic workpiece at a cutting speed of 4 m/s. The orthogonal rake angle of the cutting tool is 0°5'. The uncut chip thickness and width of cut are 0.2 mm and 3 mm, respectively. In this turning operation, the resulting friction angle and shear angle are 0°45' and 0°25', respectively. If the dynamic yield shear strength of the workpiece material under this cutting condition is 1000 MPa, then the cutting force is _______ N (round off to one decimal place).

**Solution**

First, calculate the friction angle:

$$ \tan(\phi) = \frac{\cos(5^\circ)}{\sin(25^\circ)} $$

Using a calculator, we find $\phi \approx 14.4^\circ$.

Next, apply the cutting force formula:

$$ F_c = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 0.2 \cdot \tan(14.4^\circ) \cdot 1000 $$

Simplifying and rounding to one decimal place gives $F_c \approx 2573.40$ N.

### Example 2

In a turning operation, the cutting tool has a rake angle of 10°. The width of cut is 5 mm, and the shear angle is 30°. If the dynamic yield shear strength of the workpiece material is 1200 MPa, calculate the cutting force when the uncut chip thickness is 0.1 mm.

**Solution**

Calculate the friction angle:

$$ \tan(\phi) = \frac{\cos(10^\circ)}{\sin(30^\circ)} $$

Using a calculator, we find $\phi \approx 12.9^\circ$.

Apply the cutting force formula:

$$ F_c = \frac{5}{2} \cdot 0.1 \cdot \tan(12.9^\circ) \cdot 1200 $$

Simplifying gives $F_c \approx 1874.29$ N.

**Common Pitfalls**
------------------

When solving problems related to orthogonal cutting, be cautious of the following common pitfalls:

* Misinterpreting the relationship between friction angle and shear angle
* Forgetting to convert angles from degrees to radians (if necessary)
* Incorrectly applying formulas or neglecting to use units
