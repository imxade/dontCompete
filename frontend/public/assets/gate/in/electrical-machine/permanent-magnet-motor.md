**Permanent Magnet Motor**
==========================

### Introduction
A permanent magnet motor (PMM) is an electric motor that uses a permanent magnet as its rotor, eliminating the need for a separate winding and reducing energy losses. PMMs are widely used in various applications due to their high efficiency, reliability, and compact design.

### Core Concepts
The fundamental principle of a PMM is based on the interaction between the magnetic field generated by the permanent magnet and the electromagnetic field created by the stator windings. The key concepts involved are:

*   **Permanent Magnet**: A material with a fixed magnetic moment, used as the rotor in PMMs.
*   **Stator Winding**: A set of coils placed around the stator, which generates an electromagnetic field when excited by an AC supply.
*   **Magnetic Flux Density**: The measure of the magnetic field strength at a given point.

### Key Formulas/Theorems
The following formulas are essential for understanding PMMs:

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} $$

where $B$ is the magnetic flux density, $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space, $I$ is the current flowing through the winding, and $r$ is the radius of the winding.

For a toroidal core:

$$ B = \frac{N I}{A} $$

where $B$ is the magnetic flux density, $N$ is the number of turns, $I$ is the current flowing through the winding, and $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the core.

### Examples with Solutions
**Example 1:** A PMM has a toroidal core with an inner diameter of 10 cm and an outer diameter of 14 cm. The thickness of the core is 2 cm, and it contains 200 turns of copper wire. When a current of 5 mA flows through the winding, find the magnetic flux density in the core.

Solution:

First, calculate the mean radius ($r_m$) of the toroidal core:

$$ r_m = \frac{D_i + D_o}{2} - t $$

where $D_i$ is the inner diameter, $D_o$ is the outer diameter, and $t$ is the thickness.

$$ r_m = \frac{10\text{ cm} + 14\text{ cm}}{2} - 2\text{ cm} = 6.5\text{ cm} $$

Next, calculate the magnetic flux density using the formula:

$$ B = \frac{N I}{A} $$

The cross-sectional area ($A$) of the core is given by:

$$ A = \pi (r_m)^2 - \pi \left(\frac{D_i}{2}\right)^2 $$

Substitute the values and calculate $B$.

**Example 2:** A PMM has a permanent magnet with a magnetic moment ($m$) of 100 A$\cdot$m^2. When an AC supply is connected to the stator winding, the magnetic field generated induces a voltage across the winding. Find the induced voltage if the frequency of the AC supply is 50 Hz and the number of turns is 500.

Solution:

First, calculate the angular frequency ($\omega$) using the formula:

$$ \omega = 2\pi f $$

where $f$ is the frequency.

Next, use Faraday's law to find the induced voltage ($e$):

$$ e = -N \frac{d\Phi}{dt} $$

The magnetic flux ($\Phi$) generated by the permanent magnet can be calculated using:

$$ \Phi = m B $$

Substitute the values and calculate $e$.

### Common Pitfalls
Be careful when:

*   Using incorrect units or dimensions.
*   Neglecting losses (e.g., copper losses, iron losses).
*   Misapplying formulas or laws.
*   Failing to consider the effects of saturation in the core material.
