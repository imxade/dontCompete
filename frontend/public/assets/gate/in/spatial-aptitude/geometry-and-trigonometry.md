# Geometry and Trigonometry
==========================

## Introduction
---------------

Geometry and trigonometry are fundamental components of spatial aptitude, used extensively in computer science to solve problems related to shapes, positions, distances, and angles. Understanding these concepts is crucial for tackling various types of questions on the GATE CS exam.

## Core Concepts
-----------------

### Basic Geometry Definitions

*   **Point**: A location in space.
*   **Line**: An infinite set of points extending infinitely in two directions.
*   **Angle**: The measure of the amount of rotation between two lines or planes that intersect.
*   **Plane**: A flat surface extending infinitely in all directions.

### Trigonometry Basics

*   **Sine (sin)**: Opposite side / Hypotenuse
*   **Cosine (cos)**: Adjacent side / Hypotenuse
*   **Tangent (tan)**: Opposite side / Adjacent side

## Key Formulas/Theorems
-------------------------

```math
\overline{AB}^2 + \overline{BC}^2 = \overline{AC}^2  \quad \text{(Pythagorean Theorem)}
```

### Trigonometric Identity:

```math
\sin(\theta) = \cos(90^\circ - \theta)
```

## Problem Solving Patterns
---------------------------

*   **Identify Symmetry**: Many shapes have inherent symmetries, use this to simplify calculations.
*   **Apply Geometric Principles**: Familiarize yourself with properties of basic shapes like triangles, circles, and polygons.

-----------------------------

### Example 2:

In a quadrilateral, if the opposite angles are equal and the adjacent sides are perpendicular, what is the shape's name?

Solution:
The description fits a rectangle. A rectangle has four right angles (90 degrees each) and its diagonals bisect each other.

## Common Pitfalls
-----------------

*   **Falling into Trigonometric Trap**: Be cautious of using trigonometric functions without ensuring the triangle is indeed right-angled or that you're working with acute or obtuse angles.
*   **Misapplication of Geometric Principles**: Always ensure your understanding and application of geometric properties are correct, as these are fundamental to solving problems.
