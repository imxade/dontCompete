**Electric Field Intensity and Capacitance**
=============================================

**Introduction**
---------------

Electric field intensity and capacitance are fundamental concepts in electricity and magnetism. Understanding these principles is crucial for solving problems involving electric fields, capacitors, and more. In this theory note, we will cover the key concepts, formulas, and problem-solving strategies required to tackle questions like those seen in the GATE CS exam.

**Core Concepts**
-----------------

### Electric Field Intensity

The electric field intensity ($E$) is a measure of the force exerted by an electric field on a unit charge. It is defined as the force per unit charge:

$$E = \frac{F}{q}$$

where $F$ is the force and $q$ is the charge.

### Capacitance

Capacitance ($C$) is a measure of a capacitor's ability to store electric charge. It is defined as the ratio of the charge stored on one plate to the potential difference between the plates:

$$C = \frac{Q}{V}$$

**Key Formulas/Theorems**
-------------------------

### Electric Field Intensity in Spherical Coordinates

For a point charge $q$ at the origin, the electric field intensity at a distance $r$ is given by:

$$E = \frac{k q}{r^2}$$

where $k$ is Coulomb's constant.

### Capacitance of Parallel Plates

The capacitance of parallel plates is given by:

$$C = \epsilon_0 \frac{A}{d}$$

where $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of free space, $A$ is the area of the plates, and $d$ is the distance between them.

**Problem Solving Patterns**
---------------------------

### Analyzing Capacitance Problems

When solving capacitance problems, follow these steps:

1. Identify the type of capacitor (parallel plates, spherical shells, etc.)
2. Determine the charge stored on each plate
3. Calculate the potential difference between the plates using the formula $V = \frac{Q}{C}$
4. Use the capacitance formula to find the value of $C$

**Examples with Solutions**
---------------------------

### Example 1: Capacitance of Parallel Plates

A parallel-plate capacitor has an area of $10^{-4}$ m$^2$ and a distance between plates of $10^{-3}$ m. The permittivity of free space is $\epsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}$ F/m.

$$C = \epsilon_0 \frac{A}{d} = (8.85 \times 10^{-12}) \frac{10^{-4}}{10^{-3}} = 8.85 \times 10^{-13}$$

### Example 2: Electric Field Intensity in Spherical Coordinates

A point charge of $q = 5 \times 10^{-6}$ C is placed at the origin. Find the electric field intensity at a distance $r = 0.1$ m.

$$E = \frac{k q}{r^2} = \frac{(9 \times 10^9) (5 \times 10^{-6})}{(0.1)^2} = 4.5 \times 10^3 N/C$$

**Common Pitfalls**
------------------

* Failing to identify the type of capacitor or electric field configuration
* Incorrectly applying formulas for capacitance or electric field intensity
* Not considering the effects of permittivity or other medium properties
